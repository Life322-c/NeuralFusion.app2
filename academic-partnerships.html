The framework

NeuralFusion’s cognitive integration framework

Cognitive integration is a NeuralFusion term for deliberately coordinating several modes of thinking on one problem.

The framework proposes that people tend to rely on some modes of thinking more than others, and that the way those modes are coordinated may matter for how well people handle complex decisions. NeuralFusion names the pattern of poor coordination cognitive fragmentation.

These are working concepts, developed by NeuralFusion, and presented here as hypotheses to be examined rather than as established findings.

Our position in one line. A proprietary framework with a practical tool built on it, offered openly to people who can test it properly.

The model

The Four Thinking Modes Framework

Analytical

Examines information, logic, evidence and structure to support sound decisions.

Intuitive

Handles rapid pattern recognition and instinctive judgement that is not fully deliberate.

Associative

Connects ideas, generates relationships and lets concepts interact laterally.

Reflective

Examines experience, emotional influence, assumptions, decisions and meaning.

NeuralFusion does not treat these as four separate brains. The Four Thinking Modes Framework is a proprietary NeuralFusion model describing four modes of thinking that can interact within one person’s thinking. Read the full explanation.

Methodology

CFI methodology

A concise account of what the instrument is, and the questions that remain open.

What it is

  • A 40-item self-report on a frequency scale, with items interleaved and no section headers.
  • Ten of the 40 items are worded in the opposite direction to catch yes-saying.
  • Output: four mode scores and a Coordination score (each 0 to 100), plus a derived Fragmentation score. There is no overall total.
  • No bands or norms yet. Until there are enough responses, only raw scores are reported.
  • An optional, unticked consent box allows anonymised research use of a person’s answers.

Questions we would want tested

  • Internal consistency and test-retest reliability.
  • Whether the factor structure matches the four-mode model.
  • Convergent and discriminant validity against established measures.
  • Whether reported coordination relates to observed decision quality.
  • Whether structured practice changes reported coordination, and for how long.

These are open questions, not results. The full limitations are on the methodology page.

Scientific integrity

What is established, and what is not

We separate five kinds of claim and label each one, so nothing is read as stronger than it is.

Evidence status of NeuralFusion’s concepts
Proprietary framework
NeuralFusion frameworks		The Four Thinking Modes Framework, the CFI, the Core Loop, the Integration Protocol and Clarity Delta were developed by NeuralFusion. They are not independently validated.
Research hypothesis
Research hypotheses		That coordination across thinking modes relates to how well people handle complex decisions, and that structured practice can improve reported coordination. Both are untested by independent researchers.
Existing literature
Existing scientific literature		Established research on attention, cognitive load and decision-making informs the context. It does not validate the CFI.
Validated findings
Validated scientific findings		None claimed. There is no independent, peer-reviewed validation of the CFI or the framework at this time.
Ongoing work
Ongoing validation work		The platform collects optional, consented, anonymised responses that could support future analysis. No analysis results are published. A formal psychometric study has not been completed and is an open area for collaboration.

Pilots

University pilot programmes

A pilot should be small, clear and honest about what it can and cannot show.

  • A defined cohort, such as one graduate class or programme.
  • Participants take the CFI, complete the lessons and retake the CFI.
  • The programme lead sees aggregate results, never individual answers.
  • Scope, timing and any research element agreed in advance.

Ethics first. Any research use requires your institution’s ethics approval and informed consent. We will work within your requirements, and no pilot is a substitute for formal validation.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

Is the CFI clinically or psychometrically validated?

No. The CFI is an early-stage, proprietary, self-report instrument. It has not undergone independent, peer-reviewed validation, and it is not a clinical tool. Reliability, structure and convergent validity have not been established. This is exactly where we would welcome scrutiny.

Does NeuralFusion have university or research partners?

No institutional partnerships, endorsements or research collaborations are claimed on this page. We are at the stage of seeking them.

Is the founder’s publication peer reviewed?

It is published in the Zenodo repository with a DOI. We do not describe it as peer reviewed, and we do not present it as validating the CFI or the Four Thinking Modes Framework. See the Research page.

Could the CFI be used in a study?

That is a conversation to have. Any research use would need to fit your institution’s ethics approval, consent requirements and data protection rules. Data access arrangements, if any, would be discussed case by case.

What would a university pilot involve?

A defined group, such as a graduate class or programme, would use a NeuralFusion team to take the CFI, complete the lessons and retake the CFI. The programme lead sees aggregate results. The scope, timing and any research element are agreed in advance.

Is there a cost?

We discuss this individually. The team product has seat-based packages, and academic arrangements can be talked through when you enquire.

Can the CFI be used for grading or admissions?

No. It is a self-report development and reflection tool. It should not be used for grading, selection, admissions or other high-stakes decisions.
