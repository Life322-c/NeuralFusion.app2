How NeuralFusion™ Measures Cognitive Performance

What the CFI™ is designed to assess, what it doesn't, how it was developed, and where its current limitations are.

NeuralFusion™ framework

The Cognitive Fragmentation Index (CFI™) is a proprietary assessment framework built by NeuralFusion™. It is not a clinical diagnosis, and it has not been independently, peer-reviewed validated as a psychological or medical instrument.

What NeuralFusion™ measures

NeuralFusion™'s assessments are built around the Four Thinking Modes Framework™: Analytical, Intuitive, Associative, and Reflective thinking. The CFI™ asks a person to self-report how their thinking behaves across 13 items spanning 5 dimensions, producing a score from 0 to 65 that gives a directional picture of how coordinated or fragmented their thinking currently is.

What NeuralFusion™ does not measure

The CFI™ does not measure intelligence, IQ, personality traits, or any clinically recognized psychological or neurological condition. It is not a substitute for professional psychological or medical assessment, and a CFI™ result should never be treated as a diagnosis.

How the Four Thinking Modes Framework™ is used

The framework gives each of the four modes a defined role in the CFI™'s scoring and in the platform's training sequence (the Core Loop). A person's CFI™ profile reflects the relative balance and coordination between these four modes, which is then used as the starting point for structured training inside NeuralFusion™.

How assessments are interpreted

A CFI™ score is a starting reference point, not a fixed label. Higher scores indicate more fragmented, uncoordinated thinking as self-reported by the person taking the assessment; lower scores indicate a more integrated process. Because it is self-report, the CFI™ reflects a person's own perception of their thinking rather than an independently observed measurement.

How the framework was developed

The CFI™ was developed by NeuralFusion™ founder Life Edet as an original, proprietary instrument, and has undergone expert content-validity review. It draws on established literature about attention, cognitive load, and decision-making where relevant, but the instrument itself, its specific items, and its scoring model are a NeuralFusion™ framework, not a peer-reviewed clinical or academic instrument.

Current limitations

  • The CFI™ is self-report and has not undergone large-scale, independent, peer-reviewed validation.
  • It is a research-stage framework: NeuralFusion™ continues to refine the instrument as more assessment data becomes available.
  • Results can vary based on a person's mood, context, and self-awareness at the time of taking the assessment.
  • It should not be used as the sole basis for clinical, medical, employment, or other high-stakes decisions.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the CFI™?

The Cognitive Fragmentation Index (CFI™) is a proprietary self-report assessment framework developed by NeuralFusion™: a 13-item instrument across 5 dimensions, scored 0 to 65, designed to give a starting picture of how coordinated or fragmented a person's thinking currently is.

What does the CFI™ NOT measure?

It is not a clinical, psychological, neurological, or medical diagnostic tool, and does not measure IQ, personality, or any clinically recognized condition. A CFI™ score is not a diagnosis.

How was the CFI™ developed?

It was developed by NeuralFusion™ as a proprietary framework built around the Four Thinking Modes Framework™ and has undergone expert content-validity review. It is a research-stage assessment framework, not a peer-reviewed clinical instrument.

What are the current limitations of the CFI™?

As a self-report instrument, it reflects a person's own perception of their thinking rather than an independently observed measurement, and has not undergone large-scale peer-reviewed validation. Results should be treated as a directional starting point, not a precise or fixed score.
