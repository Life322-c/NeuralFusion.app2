Research at NeuralFusion

Research at NeuralFusion explores how humans understand, develop, and preserve cognitive agency in an increasingly AI-mediated world.

NeuralFusion is an independent, emerging research and technology platform, not an academic institution. This page documents the founder's public research record, explains how it informs the platform, and invites researchers to examine and test the work. It is kept separate from the platform's own proprietary frameworks, which are described on the methodology page.

Research paper

Founder's paper November 2026  ·  SSRN

Cognitive Fragmentation and Integration Under Pressure: A Framework for Studying Human Decision-Making

Life Edet
Published
November 3, 2026
Platform
Social Science Research Network (SSRN)

This research introduces a framework for understanding cognitive fragmentation and integration under pressure, examining how individuals coordinate different modes of thinking during complex decision-making situations.

It explores the relationship between metacognition, cognitive flexibility, judgment, reasoning, intuition, creativity and reflection.

The framework provides a foundation for investigating how fragmented thinking patterns influence human decision-making and how stronger cognitive integration may support more deliberate and effective thinking.

This is a paper published on SSRN, an open research repository. It has not been published in a peer-reviewed journal, and the framework has not been independently validated.

Read on SSRN View DOI

What the research examines

The paper proposes a framework for studying cognitive fragmentation and integration under pressure: how people coordinate different ways of thinking when a decision is complex and the stakes or time pressure are high. It treats fragmented thinking as something that can be studied, and asks whether stronger integration supports more deliberate and effective thinking.

How the research relates to NeuralFusion

NeuralFusion is developing a Cognitive Performance Operating System informed by research into cognitive fragmentation, cognitive integration and human decision-making. The founder's research is part of the intellectual foundation guiding that development.

The relationship is intentional but specific. The paper is not a validation study of NeuralFusion's assessment methodology, and the framework has not been independently validated. It sets out the ideas and research questions that shaped the platform's frameworks, which in turn shape its tools.

The Cognitive Fragmentation Index (CFI)

The CFI is NeuralFusion's proprietary self-report instrument. It is a research and development tool, not a clinical measure.

What it is designed to investigate

How often a person reports using each of four ways of thinking (Analytical, Intuitive, Associative and Reflective) when working on a problem or decision, and how well they report coordinating them.

Relationship to cognitive fragmentation

The CFI is NeuralFusion's attempt to put the fragmentation idea into a form that can be studied. It derives a Fragmentation score from the Coordination score and the gap between the most-used and least-used modes.

Potential role in studying integration

If the instrument holds up under testing, it could help researchers examine whether stronger coordination across thinking modes relates to how people handle complex decisions, and whether structured practice changes it.

Current status

Early-stage research and development. The instrument is still being refined, there are no norms or score bands yet, and it is open to critique.

Transparency statement

The CFI has not yet been independently validated. It is a self-report instrument, it has not undergone large-scale peer-reviewed validation, and it is not a clinical diagnostic tool. Results are a directional starting point, not a precise or fixed score.

The SSRN paper is not described as validating the CFI. For items, scoring and limitations, see the CFI methodology and transparency page.

Academic collaboration

The framework and the CFI are early-stage, and they need independent scrutiny to become credible science. NeuralFusion is seeking research collaborators who can test, challenge and improve the work. No institutional partnerships or endorsements are claimed at this stage.

Who we would like to hear from

  • Cognitive scientists
  • Psychologists
  • Neuroscience researchers
  • Decision-making researchers
  • Universities and research institutions

Where collaboration could help

Psychometric validation

Independent examination of the CFI's items, reliability, factor structure and validity.

Experimental studies

Testing whether fragmentation and integration relate to decision quality under pressure.

Intervention research

Studying whether structured training can change how people coordinate their thinking.

Longitudinal research

Following how integration changes over time and whether any change persists.

Any research use would need to fit your institution's ethics approval, consent requirements and data protection rules. Data access, if any, would be agreed case by case. For the full partnership process, see Academic Partnerships.
